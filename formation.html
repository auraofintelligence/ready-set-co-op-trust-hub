Explore the community pathway

From shared purpose to a working cooperative.

The founding work is a sequence of decisions and evidence. People can help at any stage, including with research, administration, budgeting or practical service design.

01

Agree the local purpose

Which shared services will people actually use? Name the first useful project, the people it serves and a realistic first-year scope.

Keep: A service brief and a record of founder discussions.

02

Build the founding group

Identify eligible prospective members and how each would actively participate. Keep expressions of interest separate from formal applications.

Keep: Private founder list, eligibility checks and contact permissions.

03

Choose the cooperative structure

Discuss distributing or non-distributing status, shares, member commitments, surplus use and the needs of intended funders.

Keep: A recorded structure recommendation, reasons and unresolved questions.

04

Prepare the service and capital plan

Estimate start-up costs, wages, services, insurance, maintenance and reserves. Identify who bears each cost before registration.

Keep: Budget, cash-flow assumptions, funding sources and risk notes.

05

Draft the rules together

Start with the matching official model rules. Work through primary activities, active membership, board composition, meetings and financial arrangements.

Keep: Versioned rules, completed schedules and a record of member feedback.

06

Prepare the required disclosure

For a distributing cooperative, prepare the financial and participation disclosure. A non-distributing cooperative may also be directed to prepare one.

Keep: Required disclosure, supporting assumptions and adviser questions.

07

Obtain preliminary OFT approval

Submit the proposed rules and any required disclosure. Allow time for regulator questions before scheduling the formal meeting.

Keep: Submitted edition, correspondence and written approval references.

08

Hold the formation meeting

Use the approved documents, identify voting members, receive applications, adopt the rules, elect directors and appoint the registration contact.

Keep: Signed attendance, applications, certified rules and meeting minutes.

09

Lodge and confirm registration

Complete the official registration pack, lodge it within the required period and retain the regulator decision and registered rules.

Keep: Lodgement receipt, registration certificate and final registered rules.

10

Equip the cooperative to operate

Arrange its own banking, tax, insurance, records, signatories and relevant service permissions. Agree delegations and a reporting calendar.

Keep: Board decisions and evidence for each operating arrangement.

11

Accept each service and asset

For every proposed transfer, agree the parties, ownership or licence, price, consent, obligations, training and effective date.

Keep: Accepted handover schedule and the necessary agreements.

12

Review and share the learning

Compare actual service demand, finances and participation with the plan. Share useful templates with the next community by agreement.

Keep: Local review, approved public learning and next actions.

The legal steps are explained in Queensland law & rules. Each community's service choices remain its own.

Make the structure decision together.

Earlier ready SET Co-op research proposed a distributing cooperative. That remains a proposal for founding discussion. The choice affects shares, surplus, capital and some funding opportunities; it should follow the services members want to build.

Non-distributing describes how surplus is treated. It does not prevent a cooperative earning revenue or paying staff. Charity registration, tax concessions and grant eligibility each have their own requirements.

Give the founding group a shared working record.

Authorised organisers can keep separate community workspaces with formation actions, rule drafts, meeting notes, decisions, membership evidence, roles, finances, policies and handovers. Saved revisions retain the responsible organiser and the date, so a later group can understand how a proposal developed.

Use the public planning tools for an early discussion. Private group records belong in the organiser workspace. Access is restricted to authorised organisers.

A draft, a recorded approval and a regulator's registration decision mean different things. Record the relevant evidence and preserve the original operator when a service moves from Strange But True.

Help shape the first cooperative.

Bring experience from a local business, club, cultural or health organisation, training activity or community project. Together, we can choose the first services, agree how members participate and build a practical organisation that future communities can learn from.
