Notices, news & media

Make local information easier to find.

A club invitation, a changed ferry service, a film night or a useful local story should be easy to find and understand. Ready SETCO's first proposed shared project is a community noticeboard for residents, businesses, groups and visitors.

The aim is to help people prepare, check and share information on phones, websites and participating public screens. You can help us shape how the noticeboard is run and how we check material before sharing it.

Planning and prototype stage. You can explore the examples and offer to help. This website does not yet publish notices to a live screen network.

Proposed first shared project

The Straddie Noticeboard Network.

Before a notice is shared, its author would provide the details and a source or contact. A reviewer would check it, agree when it expires and make sure someone can update it or respond to a correction. Together, we would decide who takes on those roles.

Everyday notices

Event details, community invitations, service updates, visitor information and opportunities to learn or help. Each notice needs a source, contact, checked date and expiry.

Stories & local news

Reporting, interviews, photographs, films and event coverage. Check the facts, obtain publication permission and provide a way to correct the record.

Shared screens

Adapt approved material for phones, websites and participating displays. Each feed, venue and device would have an agreed publisher and operating arrangement.

Try the noticeboard examples and tools

You can try the noticeboard examples and prepare a draft on your device now. Publishing to shared screens is still to be built, and organisations listed in the research have not necessarily agreed to take part.

When plans change

Useful every day. Helpful during disruption.

The same noticeboard could help people find checked updates and assistance during bad weather, transport changes or recovery after a disaster. The planning project called Aura Disaster Resilience explores that part of the service.

An update would show its original source, issue time, when it was last checked and when it expires. A saved copy shown while a device is offline would be labelled, so readers can see how old it is.

Read the disruption and recovery plans

Clear sources and responsibilities.

Emergency warnings and directions retain their official issuer and meaning. Responsible emergency services decide and direct the response. The community network's proposed role is to help people receive, understand and act on reliable information.

Research, demonstrations and future field trials each need their own visible status.

Local news, film and storytelling

Make local stories, and learn the craft.

There are island stories worth telling and people who would enjoy learning to record them. Interviews, photographs, short films and screenings could bring local experience to a wider audience. The Minjerribah Screen & Media Network gathers ideas and resources for developing that work together.

From an idea to a story people can share.

A useful story starts with listening. Check the facts with the people involved, agree what can be shared and prepare it for its audience. Someone should be responsible for the final version and available if a correction is needed.

Start with one small task.

You could try a phone interview, help with captions, check a notice or support a screening. Roles would have agreed pay or be clearly voluntary. If you choose to volunteer, the proposed C-Hour system would recognise each verified human hour without putting a money value on it. Paid work keeps its wages and conditions.

Practical capture toolkit

One story, ready for different screens.

A small, well-prepared shoot can provide a short phone story, a wide-screen version and useful evidence of the activity.

Plan the footage and permissions

Capture what explains the activity.

  • A wide view of the place, with location permission
  • Hands, materials and the task in progress
  • A clear sound recording or short interview
  • The finished result and a plain explanation
  • Source, date, credits and publication permission

Prepare both formats.

Frame a vertical 9:16 version for phones and a landscape 16:9 version for screens. Add readable captions and keep essential details understandable without sound.

Keep private paperwork, screens and sensitive locations out of shot. Images of children, personal stories and cultural material require the appropriate permission and authority.

Useful first story ideas
  • A short invitation explaining what Ready SETCO is proposing
  • A beginner lesson in checking the source of a notice
  • A local participant explaining an activity they want to develop
  • An event setup, screening or equipment-care demonstration
  • A completed activity and what the team learned from it
