Formation & governance · Queensland law & rules

Know the steps.
Know who decides.

Queensland cooperatives register through the Office of Fair Trading. Founders prepare a viable purpose and rulebook, obtain the required preliminary approvals, hold a formation meeting and apply for registration. These are separate steps with separate records.

Local people. Shared responsibility.

Queensland is the first jurisdiction covered here. The public planner helps prepare questions and documents; the Office of Fair Trading makes the registration and approval decisions.

The legal framework

The Co-operatives National Law Act 2020 applies the national scheme in Queensland. Read it with the Co-operatives National Law (Queensland), applicable national regulations and Queensland's local regulation. Registered rules add the cooperative's own choices within that framework.

Keep the source edition and review date with each rule or policy draft. A model downloaded years ago may need checking against current requirements.

Type, membership and economic participation

Start by choosing a distributing cooperative or one of the two non-distributing configurations. A distributing cooperative has share capital; a non-distributing cooperative may have shares or operate without them. A primary cooperative generally needs at least five suitably qualified founders and must maintain five active members, unless OFT approves a smaller number. Formation participants who are individuals must be adults. Confirm eligibility and any exceptional structure with OFT.

The rules need to connect membership to the cooperative's actual activities. In a primary cooperative, each eligible member has one vote; buying more shares does not add votes. Expressing interest, donating or using a website does not establish membership.

Official guidance: Cooperative types and minimum membership.

Rules and active membership

Use the matching official model rulebook as the starting document. Complete the local details and discuss the choices before submitting it. The rules need to address purpose, member participation, admission, governance and finance.

Submit proposed rules at least 28 days before the intended formation meeting. Wait for approval before holding it. If founders change the approved text at that meeting, it must return to OFT and another formation meeting will be needed.

Keep consultation notes alongside the draft, with a clear record of which edition was approved and which was adopted.

Disclosure before people commit

A distributing cooperative needs a formation disclosure statement for OFT approval. The Registrar can also require one from a non-distributing cooperative. It should let prospective members understand the business, their commitments, expected benefits and risks before deciding to join.

Build the supporting financial assumptions alongside the service plan. Distinguish committed finance from an application, forecast or hopeful future revenue. Disclose relevant interests and proposed arrangements with founders or related businesses.

Official guidance: Preparing a distributing cooperative disclosure.

Formation meeting and registration

Follow OFT's meeting order: identify and obtain signatures from intending voting members; present required disclosure; receive membership applications; vote on the approved rules; elect directors; and appoint the person who will complete registration.

Under section 22, at least two-thirds of the proposed members attending the formation meeting must support the approved rules. Keep attendance and vote counts, including abstentions. Lodge the registration documents and fee within two months after the meeting closes, unless OFT grants an extension.

Keep the registration certificate and registered rules when issued. A meeting minute or a website status is not that certificate.

Official guidance: Formation procedure and the registration pack.

Official guidance: National Law, sections 22 and 26.

Directors, officers and authority

A cooperative needs at least three directors, with a majority being member directors and at least two ordinarily resident in Australia. Check the rules and law for individual eligibility, elections, quorum and appointments.

Directors oversee finances, services and the interests of the cooperative, manage conflicts, arrange meetings and keep members informed. Delegating day-to-day work should come with written limits, reporting and review.

Voting and significant changes

Use the correct procedure for the decision. Ordinary resolutions generally need a simple majority. A special resolution normally needs two-thirds of eligible votes cast at a general meeting or normal postal ballot; a special postal ballot needs three-quarters of formal votes cast. The rules can set a higher threshold.

Allow at least 21 days' notice for a special resolution. A special postal ballot has a separate minimum of 28 days after its notice and requires approved disclosure. Check the law and rules before choosing a voting method. Certain major asset transactions require that procedure; ordinary equipment purchases do not automatically do so.

A recorded vote is only part of the process. Check any OFT registration or approval needed before a change takes effect.

Official guidance: Resolutions, notices and ballots.

Official guidance: National Law, sections 239, 240 and 248.

Registers and member information

Maintain the required registers and records, including members, directors and applicable financial interests and securities. Preserve dates, supporting documents and authorised changes. Provide access through the rights and procedures that apply to each record.

Public transparency can use agreed reports, budgets and project updates. Personal contact details, applications and sensitive employment records need controlled access. Keep a request for information separate from a decision to publish it online.

Official guidance: Required registers.

Annual reporting and audit

Check reporting classification each year. Ordinarily, a small cooperative meets at least two consolidated tests: annual revenue below $8 million, year-end gross assets below $4 million, and fewer than 30 full-time-equivalent employees. Additional securities and share-raising conditions apply under regulation 1.4; membership size alone does not settle it.

Annual reporting to OFT is due within five months of financial year end. The first AGM is due within 18 months of registration. Thereafter, hold an AGM each calendar year and within five months of financial year end. Prepare and circulate the required member financial information.

Large cooperatives require audited reports. Small cooperatives are not automatically exempt in every situation: member requests, regulator directions or other obligations can require an audit or review.

Forms, fees and lodgement

Download the current regulator forms when preparing the application. Use the official fee schedule at the time of lodgement, and budget for both regulatory fees and any legal or accounting help.

The workbooks here help collect information. They do not submit an application, collect a government fee or replace the regulator's signed forms.

Turn the model rules into a local discussion.

The useful work is deciding how the cooperative will operate in practice. Use these questions with the official model document; keep unresolved choices visible until the founders agree them.

Purpose and primary activities

Specify what the cooperative exists to do and which measurable activities count as its primary activities.

Active membership

Define a workable minimum level of participation, how it is measured, permitted absences and the lawful process for inactivity.

Admission and exit

Who may apply, who decides, what must be paid or agreed, and how resignations, refunds, cancellations and disputes are handled.

Shares, fees and finance

Identify share subscriptions if applicable, joining or service fees, funding limits, reserves, surplus policy and financial records.

Member meetings and voting

Set lawful notice, quorum, representation, voting, meeting records and resolution procedures.

Directors and officers

Set board size and eligibility, elections, terms, vacancies, conflicts, duties and delegated authority.

Disputes and review

Provide a fair, accessible process for raising concerns, resolving disputes and reviewing decisions.

Changes and winding up

Use the legal process for rule changes, structural changes and the final treatment of liabilities and remaining assets.

For ready SET Co-op, discuss how businesses, community organisations, workers and other eligible participants meet the primary activities. Membership categories need to fit the law and preserve the cooperative's democratic rules.
