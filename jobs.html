Working holiday work on Straddie

Looking for your 88 days?

We want to help backpackers connect with properly paid tourism, hospitality and guest-services opportunities as they develop locally. You can register your interest now; this is an enquiry, rather than a job offer or a promise of visa eligibility.

Register for working holiday work

Straddie's postcode, 4183, is listed for eligible tourism and hospitality work. The actual industry, duties, pay, dates and evidence still matter. Check the Home Affairs rules for subclass 417 or subclass 462.

  1. Check which rules apply to you

    Your visa, passport and application date affect the requirements. Read the rule-change note below before planning your stay.

  2. Tell us when you can work

    Share your availability, relevant experience and the paid work you are looking for. The form has a working holiday option. Keep identity documents for a later private employment process.

  3. Agree the role before starting

    Confirm the employer, duties, work location, lawful pay and roster. Keep your agreement, hours, payslips and payment records. Home Affairs decides whether the work meets its requirements.

Visa rules are changing

Checked 19 November 2026. Home Affairs says the transitional 88-day second-visa pathway requires completing the work and lodging a valid application by 28 February 2027. From 1 March 2027, it announces 120 days and an invitation for second-visa applications. UK passport arrangements are separate.

Read the official application-process guide (PDF) and check the latest Home Affairs updates.

Know your rights at work
Which work and days can count?

Home Affairs includes certain tourism and hospitality roles, including work at events and entertainment venues. We are exploring visitor support around conferences, events and island experiences. A tourism postcode or event title alone does not establish eligibility; the actual role needs checking.

The current 88-day requirement is based on the calendar equivalent of normal full-time work. Part-time or occasional shifts can take longer; working extra hours in a day does not let you finish in fewer than 88 calendar days. Use the official counting examples for your situation.

Tourism and hospitality work must be lawfully paid. The voluntary C-Hour idea explained below does not replace wages or meet this paid-work requirement. UK passport holders have separate arrangements.

C-Hours · voluntary contribution

A way to recognise voluntary help.

People already give time to care, mentoring and community activities. The proposed C-Hour approach would recognise that help by recording one C-Hour for each checked hour of voluntary human contribution, with no money value. Communities would decide how to recognise and thank contributors.

Room for what people already give.

Care, mentoring, helping a neighbour, sharing knowledge and supporting a community activity can all be part of this exploration. The description preserves what someone did, learned and helped make possible.

You choose what is shared.

You would help describe your contribution, check the record and choose whether to share it. A future personal AI assistant could help prepare that description using information you allow it to use. This website does not run that assistant or a C-Hour recording service yet.

C-Hours do not replace wages. Paid work, money and anything produced by machines are recorded separately from voluntary human time.
