Employment and training

Put Luke out of one job, then turn the pattern into many jobs.

The 300+ jobs aim is not a slogan. It needs a ladder from sole-trader proof, to teachable tasks, to local crews, to formal co-op services, to larger ecosystem roles.

1-3 year target

Big enough to matter, staged enough to stay believable.

The early numbers should be treated as planning ranges and revised with real demand, funding, insurance, licences and partner capacity.

30early helpers

People trained around immediate local work: tech help, events, media, admin, grant evidence and small business support.

100repeatable roles

Part-time, casual, project, volunteer-to-paid and micro-contract roles once checklists and quality standards exist.

300+local job pathways

Possible 1-3 year horizon across co-op services, hyperlocal media, events, training, sport, assets, resilience and partner projects.

Phasing

Do not wait for a perfect institution before useful work starts.

Ready S.E.T. Co-op can start as a service pattern before it becomes a mature legal, labour-hire or training structure. The public site should make that sequence obvious.

Phase 0: Sole-trader proof

Strange but True delivers immediate paid services and writes down what repeats.

Phase 1: Local helper bench

Train people to handle parts of the work: forms, devices, captions, event setup, content checks, grant notes and asset registers.

Phase 2: Crew and supervisor roles

Group repeatable tasks into crews with a clear lead, quality checks and basic public evidence.

Phase 3: Co-op services

Offer structured services to businesses and clubs: HR admin support, training coordination, media packages, compliance notes and asset systems.

Phase 4: Formal engine

Move toward distributing co-operative membership, labour hire licensing, RTO partnerships and service agreements only when readiness is real.

Role families

The first job families are practical, visible and trainable.

Keep the first public language grounded. The future tech roles can appear later once people can see the plain service base.

Digital confidenceDevice and AI helpers

Phone setup, account recovery, scam awareness, AI basics, forms, browser use and patient one-on-one support.

Business profiles, service listings, social posts, basic websites, booking pages, quote folders and plain-English SOPs.

Photos, attendance logs, outcomes, partner lists, quotes, budget notes, source links and grant profile updates.

Welcoming, triage, conflict-aware facilitation, newcomer orientation, workshop hosting and handover to the right person.

Legal infoSource trail assistants

Collect official links, dates, forms and document names for later qualified review. No legal advice.

Deep skills laterDigital custodians and builders

Digital twins, local AI, credential systems, data stewardship and simulation skills belong after the first trust and training layers.

Putting Luke out of work

A handoff is a success, not a loss.

Every time Luke changes projects, the goal is to leave behind a working pattern that no longer depends on him personally. That means the site needs role descriptions, media training, repeatable builders, checklists, public links and a way to thank contributors without making private life public.

This is how a sole-trader seed becomes a co-op pathway: do the task, name the pattern, train someone, document the boundary, publish the public-safe version, then move the bottleneck to the next unsolved layer.

Job creation test

If a task can be taught, checked, paid for, handed over and improved, it can become a role. If it only lives in Luke's head, it is still a bottleneck.
