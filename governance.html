Formation & governance · Running the cooperative

Members choose.
People carry the work.

Good governance makes everyday work clearer: who can decide, what has been agreed, who is responsible and how members can ask questions. Set these arrangements up while forming the cooperative, then review them as its services grow.

Local people. Shared responsibility.

ready SET Co-op is still proposed. The arrangements below are a preparation guide. Actual authority comes from the law, registered rules, member decisions and properly recorded appointments.

Keep the membership journey clear.

Interest

An enquiry opens a conversation. Record the contact permission and the person or organisation making it.

Application

Provide the rules, eligibility and commitments. Keep the application and the authority for an organisation representative.

Admission

Record the actual decision and date, applicable payments or shares, and the member register entry.

Active participation

Check participation against the rules. Use the proper notice and decision process for changes, inactivity or exit.

An employment role, a supplier contract, volunteering and cooperative membership are distinct relationships. A person may have more than one, with different responsibilities and records.

Technical access does not confer a member vote or board authority. A webmaster can maintain the system while a properly appointed person remains responsible for the recorded decision.

Official guidance: Directors and officers.

Make decisions understandable later.

  1. State the proposal

    Describe the service, change or expenditure, who benefits, costs, alternatives and the people affected.

  2. Identify the authority

    Record the relevant rule, decision-maker, notice requirements, eligibility, quorum and any conflicts.

  3. Record the decision

    Keep the exact resolution, documents considered, vote counts or delegated approval, result and evidence.

  4. Follow through

    Name the person responsible, budget limit, effective date and review date. Record changes as new revisions.

Check the Queensland voting requirements

For example, accepting a Noticeboard service needs a clear service scope, operating costs, rights to software and content, and the receiving cooperative's agreement. An interesting proposal can stay open while that evidence is gathered.

Keep trading, capital and contribution distinct.

Trading and employment

Track service revenue and operating costs. Name the legal employer, agree duties and pay, and use appropriate employment and payroll arrangements.

Member capital and surplus

Keep share subscriptions, loans, fees and retained reserves identifiable. Apply the chosen cooperative structure and approved financial decisions.

Grants and restricted funds

Check each programme and applicant. Track funding conditions, eligible spending, acquittals and who is accountable.

Voluntary contribution

One verified C-Hour recognises an hour of freely given human help without a monetary equivalent. It does not replace pay, share capital or a member vote.

For a proposed equipment or compute installation, plan maintenance, energy, connectivity, training, insurance and renewal as well as the purchase price. Keep the cost assumptions open to review by the people who will operate it.

Build a rhythm people can sustain.

At each decision

Check authority and interests; record the decision, accountable person and follow-up.

Regularly, as agreed locally

Review cash flow, service quality, workload, active participation, actions, access and backup recovery.

Each financial year

Confirm reporting classification, prepare member reports, arrange the AGM and lodge required annual information.

When circumstances change

Review appointments, rule changes, contracts, insurance, membership and the authority for new services.

Use the dates and reporting requirements in the OFT financial reporting guide to set the cooperative's calendar.

Transfer services with their history intact.

Strange But True is the initial operator. A future cooperative can accept defined work through agreed arrangements. For each item, specify the original and receiving parties, ownership or licence, costs, permissions, liabilities, support and effective date.

Equipment might be sold, leased, licensed or contributed. Software and content need clear rights. Contact information needs an appropriate sharing basis. Employment and grant obligations need their own review. A shared website does not move these arrangements by itself.

Retain the original records and make the board's acceptance easy to find. Test that the receiving team has the training, access and support needed to continue the service.

Prepare a service and asset handover schedule

Let members ask and improve things.

Provide a clear route for questions, corrections, concerns and disputes. Record the issue, responsible person and response date, and follow the cooperative's rules. Share approved updates in plain language so members can understand both progress and unresolved problems.

Publish only information that has been approved for public use. Sensitive applications, personnel information and private contacts remain in controlled records.
