Formation & governance · Cooperatives in other communities

Start on Straddie.
Make the foundation reusable.

A community needs more than equipment to turn an idea into useful work. It needs people, skills, services, funding and a way to make decisions together. ready SET Co-op is designed to test that foundation on Minjerribah, then make the useful parts available to other places.

Local people. Shared responsibility.

The wider ambition is to support up to 3,000 potential Missing Middle sites. This is a proposed scale for the network, not a list of established cooperatives, secured locations or funded installations.

Repeat the process. Choose the purpose locally.

Another community could begin with training, shared administration, a noticeboard, equipment, a workshop or a compute service. Its founders would identify the local need, choose the legal structure, develop rules and decide which projects to take on.

A physical site, a cooperative and a shared service are different things. One cooperative might operate several facilities, and one facility might involve several organisations. A postcode can help people find one another without fixing the boundary of their membership or authority.

Reusable foundations

Formation prompts, source references, record formats, meeting workbooks, budgets, handover schedules and lessons from delivery.

Local choices

Members, rules, services, cultural relationships, funding, property, workforce, ownership and the pace of growth.

Agreed connections

Shared training, procurement, support or federation arrangements, with written scope, costs, authority and a way to leave.

Connect people to useful infrastructure.

The Installing the Missing Middle proposal explores distributed computing and other productive community infrastructure. A ready SET cooperative could provide the local employment, training and stewardship needed to put an installation to work.

Formation can start while technical and financial feasibility is being assessed. A cooperative would still need an agreed facility, power, connectivity, equipment plan, operating budget, trained people and appropriate permissions before accepting an installation.

The wider research includes pathways in which assets move into local ownership over time. Any progressive purchase, financing margin, lease or service arrangement needs clear pricing, title, liabilities, member approval and an exit process before commitments are made.

Useful early outcomes can be modest: a trained team, a service that people use, a sustainable budget and a record of decisions. Those are foundations for later capacity.

Prepare another community in six connected steps.

  1. Listen locally

    Identify people who want to work together, the services they need, and existing organisations with relevant authority or experience.

  2. Check the jurisdiction

    Find the current local regulator, applicable law, model rules, fees and reporting requirements. Queensland guidance needs local review before use elsewhere.

  3. Choose a feasible service

    Start with a bounded offer, demand evidence, costs, skills and the people accountable for delivery.

  4. Form the organisation

    Use the appropriate formation process and keep each local cooperative's applications, approvals, membership and decisions distinct.

  5. Agree the shared services

    Document any network support, software rights, procurement, training, data exchange and dispute or exit arrangements.

  6. Share what works

    Contribute approved templates and practical lessons. Remove personal records and obtain permission for any local or cultural material.

Joining a wider network should have an understandable purpose and agreement. It does not automatically transfer local ownership, member records, cultural authority or control of private information.

Keep records useful wherever the system runs.

The working foundation keeps community identities separate, uses versioned records and provides private exports. A new community starts with its own decisions and participants. Blank prompts can travel; another cooperative's membership, consents, appointments and approval history cannot be inherited as local facts.

The existing Cloudflare service is the initial home. A later move to local infrastructure needs an authorised team, secure access, backup and restore checks, and a clear transfer date. Hosting and legal ownership can change at different times.
