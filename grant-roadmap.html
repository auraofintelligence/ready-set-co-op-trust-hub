Grants & project support

A good idea needs a workable plan.

Your club might need equipment, a group might want to run a workshop, or an event might need help getting off the ground. Finding funding means turning that idea into a plan someone can assess: who it helps, what it costs and who will carry it out.

Ready SETCO proposes bringing people together to share that preparation. Bring the idea you have and the help you need, and a local team could work through the application with you.

Ask about a project or funding ideaSee the preparation steps

The support team is still being formed. You can express interest in getting help or bringing your own experience to it.

Start with the purpose

Make the local benefit clear.

A short description of what people want to do, who it would help and why gives everyone a starting point. The people forming the cooperative would choose which projects it supports.

  1. Describe the need

    Who would benefit, what have they asked for, and what is the smallest useful result?

  2. Work out who can apply

    Check the funder's rules to see which person or organisation can apply, then agree who would hold the funds and do the work. Organisations should agree to take part before being named as partners.

  3. Build a realistic budget

    Include people, access, equipment, insurance, permissions, maintenance and reporting. Keep quotes and assumptions with the figures.

  4. Check the actual funding round

    Use the funder's current guidelines for eligibility, deadlines, permitted costs and assessment. A possible match is a lead to investigate.

Build evidence as you go

Keep one useful project folder.

Bring together the brief, decision record, budget, quotes, permissions, delivery dates and the person responsible for each next step. Record what happened and what people learned.

Photos, attendance and participant stories need appropriate permission. Publish an agreed summary, with private working material kept separately.

Before submitting

  • A clear local purpose and agreed scope.
  • A confirmed applicant and authorised sign-off.
  • Evidence for the need and budget.
  • Delivery responsibilities and reporting dates.
  • Current guidelines and a final human review.

Research to build on

Find a starting point for your application.

Luke Hayes has gathered local project research, funding links and preparation tools on the Stradbroke Grants Lab website. They can help you collect an organisation's details and explore possible funding. Each funding link leads back to the funder's current rules and dates.

Collect the organisation's facts, purpose, capacity and supporting documents.

Explore funding sources

Plan the public story

Share agreed notices, news and project outcomes through the local network.

The research provides possible funding leads. An actual application would need an eligible applicant, their agreement and a check against the current funding round.
