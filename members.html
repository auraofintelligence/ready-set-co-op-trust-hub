Members & decisions

Help build a cooperative that locals can own.

Helping form Ready SETCO means deciding what the cooperative should do, who can join and how we make decisions together. Local businesses, community organisations and community leaders are invited to bring their experience to those choices.

The founding group would develop clear membership terms, responsibilities and ways to review decisions. You can help with that work or enquire about an activity before choosing whether membership is right for you.

Where we are now

You might want help with an event, a chance to learn, or a way to contribute a skill. The Take part form puts you in touch with Luke Hayes, who is starting this work through his business Strange But True. He receives the enquiry privately and follows up about what could happen next.

Local choice

Build around what people need.

Finding local events, services and useful updates is one place to begin. The proposed Straddie Noticeboard Network would bring that information together on phones and shared screens. Events, learning and practical support are other possibilities to discuss.

Luke's research offers starting ideas. As we form Ready SETCO, we would agree its rules and priorities, with members choosing the commitments it takes on. Each proposed project would need its own decision and the agreement of anyone involved.

A useful decision record

  • What is proposed and who would benefit?
  • Who has a say and who holds responsibility?
  • What will it cost, and who has agreed to help?
  • What was decided, by whom and when?
  • What happened, and when do we review it?

These are proposed foundations. The detailed decision process belongs in the rules members develop.

From one business to a shared organisation

Pass the work to a local team.

The initial enquiries are held by Luke's sole trader business, Strange But True. Once Ready SETCO is registered, the business and cooperative could agree which services, equipment and responsibilities it is ready to take on.

That handover would include the training, permissions and records the new team needs to continue the work, along with clear agreement about costs and responsibilities.

  1. Agree what moves

    Identify the service, assets, ongoing costs, contracts and people responsible. Record the cooperative's acceptance.

  2. Respect each person's permission

    The participation form asks separately about future sharing with the registered cooperative. Contact details move only through the agreed consent and handover process.

  3. Keep the history

    Record the original operator, the receiving legal entity and the effective date. Existing work and obligations keep their proper history.

  4. Support the new team

    Pass on instructions, access, training and maintenance knowledge so the service can continue under its agreed responsibility.

Different ways to contribute

Recognise the work people put in.

Someone might take a paid role, share equipment or choose to help with a community activity. Each arrangement needs to be clear. The proposed C-Hour record would recognise verified hours of voluntary human help, with no money value; paid roles keep their own wages and conditions.

People could also share what they learned and what the project achieved, while choosing what stays private. Machine activity would be recorded separately from human time.
