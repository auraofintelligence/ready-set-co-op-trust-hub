Sources & further reading

Follow the work that brought us here.

Ready SETCO grows out of Luke Hayes's work on local employment, learning, information and shared resources. If something here catches your interest, these links take you further into the projects and papers behind it.

They open separate websites, including research and examples you can try. As we form the cooperative, we can choose which ideas to take forward together.

Ideas close to home

Community notices, local stories and help with funding all have a part in this work. Follow whichever connection interests you.

Explore Luke's local work

The Strange But True Community Ledger gathers local projects and ways people could contribute. It helps explain the work that led to this invitation to build a team.

Explore earlier local research

Earlier research brought local knowledge and public-information ideas together in the Ready SET Co-op Cultural Intelligence Node. The collection gives more background to the community work proposed here.

Share useful community notices

The Straddie Noticeboard Network is the first proposed shared project: checked notices on phones, websites and public screens. The existing website and notice builder are examples to try, rather than a live publishing network.

Try the noticeboard examples

Find information when plans change

Aura Disaster Resilience explores how the same community information service could help during disruption and recovery. It is design and planning work; local trials and operating arrangements still need to be developed.

Read the disruption and recovery plans

Tell local stories and learn media skills

The Minjerribah Screen & Media Network website gathers plans and resources for reporting, filmmaking, screenings and sharing local stories. It is a starting point for exploring the work and skills involved.

The Stradbroke Grants Lab brings together local project research, funding links and tools for gathering an organisation's details. Check the funder's own current information before relying on a grant or deadline.

Optional wider reading

These publications explore how communities could share the benefits of technology fairly. They provide ideas to consider, not a programme members have already agreed to.

Recognise voluntary help

People already give their time to neighbours and community activities. The C-Hour proposal would recognise each verified hour of that human contribution, with no money value. People would choose what to share, while paid work keeps its own wages and conditions.

Read how C-Hours would work

Support the steps between an idea and delivery

Shared computing equipment needs people who can run it, training, funding and ongoing care. Installing the Missing Middle brings those practical steps into a longer-term proposal for community computing and local ownership.

Follow the connection to Ready SETCO

Further reading: public inquiries and policy contributions

Luke has put related ideas to inquiries and public discussions. These papers explain the reasoning behind some proposals here. A published submission records what was requested; it does not mean the government or a committee adopted it.

Funding local services and recognising community help

The local-government funding and fiscal sustainability submission asks for legal changes and a local pilot for C-Hours, alongside community records that protect privacy. The proposed legal carve-out and testing arrangements are policy requests, rather than an exemption available today.

Read the published parliamentary submission (PDF)

Skills, resilience and national security

The AUKUS paper asks how local training, community computing and resilience could contribute to national security. It was prepared for the independent AUKUS Public Inquiry, rather than a parliamentary committee inquiry.

Read the AUKUS Public Inquiry contribution

Practical cooperation with Pacific communities

The Fiji–Australia Vuvale Union and Ocean of Peace papers propose locally chosen shared assets, training and control over community information. They connect the “missing middle” with peaceful cooperation. They are committee submissions proposing possibilities, rather than adopted programmes.

Fiji–Australia Vuvale Union submissionOcean of Peace / Veitacini Treaty submission

Control information and check its quality

Communities should be able to decide how their information is used and connect different systems on terms they agree to. The Pacific papers carry those aims into proposals for data sovereignty and fair interoperability. They also explore GAJRA Earth, the proposed Global Association for Joyful Responsible Abundance, where cultures and communities could share ideas while keeping their own voice. The De-Slop work connects everyday wellbeing and health research with improving the quality of information. These are research proposals, rather than a clinical service.

Read these ideas in the Vuvale paper
