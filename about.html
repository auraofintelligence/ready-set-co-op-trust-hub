The idea behind Ready SETCO

Local people, building useful things together.

Ready SETCO stands for Ready Sustainable Employment and Training Cooperative. It is a proposed organisation for local skills, paid work, shared resources and practical help on Minjerribah / North Stradbroke Island.

A cooperative would be owned and directed by its members. The invitation now is to help shape what it should do and build a team to carry it forward.

Ask a question or offer to helpUnderstand the idea

Why bring these things together?

An idea is easier with a team behind it.

Someone planning an event might need a venue, guest support, equipment, permission and help with a funding application. Someone seeking work might need training, an introduction and a clear paid role. The aim is to organise that support locally and share the work.

A first proposed project is a community noticeboard for events and checked local information, called the Straddie Noticeboard Network. It could help residents and visitors find what is happening and where to get help when plans change.

Luke Hayes has developed the initial ideas and is starting this work through his business Strange But True. He is inviting other people to bring their experience, help improve it and form a cooperative that members can direct.

What can I do here now?

  • Explore the ideas and ask questions.
  • Use the public map or suggest a place.
  • Say what you want to learn or what help you need.
  • Offer skills, a service, equipment or a venue.
  • Express interest in helping form the cooperative.

Events, paid roles and other services need their own confirmed arrangements. An enquiry starts a conversation.

Start small. Keep local choice.

What is a “minimum viable community”?

It means starting with enough people, shared tools and organisation to do something useful together, then improving it through use. “A fair go in the age of superintelligence” is the wider goal: as AI becomes more capable, local people should have opportunities to learn, earn and help decide how it is used.

“Fractal” describes how the useful foundations could be repeated in another place, with its own people and choices. A community in the Southern Moreton Bay Islands or Redlands could adapt what works without taking on every project proposed here.

Locally governed

Each community chooses its services, responsibilities and projects. A postcode is a useful way to find people; an island or another locally meaningful boundary may fit the work better.

Connected as peers

Communities can share tools, training, lessons and purchasing opportunities through agreed relationships. The pattern leaves room for cooperatives and other locally suitable organisations.

Improve through use

Try something useful, document what happens, change what needs changing and help the next community begin with better knowledge.

From an idea to something that works

What is the “missing middle”?

It is the practical support between having a local idea and making it happen: people to work with, training, equipment, funding and someone to help organise the steps. Ready SETCO aims to build that support so one person or small group does not have to do everything alone.

A wider proposal called Installing the Missing Middle applies this idea to community computing: local servers, training, useful heat from the equipment and a path towards local ownership. That is one possible future direction for members to consider, rather than a requirement for getting involved now.

Any equipment project would need agreed ownership, a team trained to run it, maintenance and renewal costs. People can start working together before such an installation exists.

What does that mean for this website?

It currently runs on Cloudflare, an online hosting service. The software and records are designed to be movable, so island-based servers could be considered later when there are suitable facilities and an agreed team to look after them.
