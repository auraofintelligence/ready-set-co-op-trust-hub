The idea behind Ready SETCO

Share the work. Keep the choices local.

A club running an event and a business offering training may both need equipment, guest support, permission or funding. Ready SETCO is proposed as a way to share that organising work, so useful knowledge and resources carry from one activity to the next.

Cooperative ownership would give members a say in how that support is run and what it takes on. Together, we could build lasting local capability while each participating business and organisation keeps its own identity and responsibilities.

A local team

Keep useful knowledge in the community.

Each activity could leave something others can use: instructions for equipment, a clearer booking process or someone ready to teach the next person. Keeping that knowledge with a team would help work continue when an organiser is busy or someone moves on.

The proposed Straddie Noticeboard Network offers a practical place to develop that approach: agree who prepares notices, who checks them and how to keep them current. Explore the first shared project.

Luke Hayes has developed the initial ideas and is starting this work through his business Strange But True. He is inviting you to bring your experience, improve the ideas and help form a cooperative we can carry forward together.

There is a place to begin.

  • Explore the ideas and ask questions.
  • Use the public map or suggest a place.
  • Say what you want to learn or what help you need.
  • Offer skills, a service, equipment or a venue.
  • Express interest in helping form the cooperative.

Start with an enquiry. Any event, paid role or other activity would have its own agreed arrangements.

Start small. Keep local choice.

Build something useful, then build on it.

A few people, some shared tools and a manageable activity are enough to begin learning together. Run it, find out what helps and leave the next person a better starting point. This is the small, working beginning Luke calls a minimum viable community.

As AI becomes more capable, local people should have a fair chance to learn, earn and help decide how it is used. Working towards a fair go in the age of superintelligence can begin with those everyday opportunities.

Other communities across the Southern Moreton Bay Islands, Redlands and beyond could adapt what works here, choosing their own activities and sharing what they learn in return.

Local people choose

The people involved choose the work and take responsibility for it. An island, neighbourhood or another area that makes sense locally can be the place to start.

Share what works

Communities could share equipment, training and purchasing opportunities while keeping their own organisations and decisions.

Learn together

Keep the useful instructions, talk about what needs improving and help the next person or community get started.

Help with the steps in between

Give a good idea the support to happen.

A club might know exactly what it wants to do and still need equipment, training, funding or someone to help organise it. Ready SETCO would bring that support closer to home, with a team to share the work and help people carry it through.

That gap between an idea and the people and tools needed to deliver it runs through Luke's wider proposal, Installing the Missing Middle. It explores how communities could eventually own computing equipment, train people to run it and put its otherwise wasted heat to use.

For now, people can start working together. Any later equipment project would need members to agree the ownership, costs and people responsible for looking after it.

Room for local ownership

This website currently runs on Cloudflare. Its software and records can be moved, leaving room for island-based servers later if there are suitable facilities and a local team to look after them.
